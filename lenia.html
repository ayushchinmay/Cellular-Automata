How Lenia works

Conway's Life with the discreteness taken out — of space, of time, and of state.

1 · State is continuous

Every cell holds a real number A ∈ [0,1] instead of dead/alive. The world is a torus: everything wraps.

2 · The neighbourhood becomes a kernel

Life counts 8 neighbours. Lenia takes a weighted average over a disc of radius R using a ring-shaped kernel that sums to 1, where weight depends only on distance r/R:

K(r) = β⌊rB⌋ · core((rB) mod 1),   core(u) = exp(4 − 1/(u(1−u)))

One ring gives a single fuzzy annulus; β = [1, ⅔, ⅓] gives three rings of decreasing weight. The ring recipe decides which species you get.

3 · Potential

Convolve world with kernel. Each cell learns how crowded its neighbourhood is at the scale the kernel cares about:

U = K ∗ A

4 · Growth

"Born on 3, survive on 2–3" becomes a smooth bell centred at μ with width σ, mapping potential to change in [−1, +1]:

G(u) = 2·exp(−(u−μ)² / 2σ²) − 1

Just right → grow. Too sparse or too crowded → shrink. σ is the tolerance and the most sensitive knob in the system.

5 · Time is continuous too

A ← clip(A + (1/T)·G(K ∗ A), 0, 1)

At T = 10 a cell needs ten steps to cross its full range, so structures deform instead of flickering.

Why anything interesting happens

The kernel makes a cell want a specific amount of mass at a specific distance. A ring of the right thickness feeds its inner edge while starving its outer edge, so it advances; break the symmetry and it turns. What emerges are solitons — self-repairing, self-propelling blobs that survive perturbation, collide, split and orbit. Nobody programmed them; they are the only stable solutions.

Conway is in here

A 3×3 kernel with the centre at half weight, hard step growth, T = 1 and a binary start reproduces the Game of Life exactly, cell for cell. That's the Conway limit preset.

Scale

R and T scale together: doubling both gives roughly the same creature at twice the size, more finely resolved. That's how the small discovered rules were grown to fit a 256 world.
