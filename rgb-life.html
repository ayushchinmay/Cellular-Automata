What this is

Conway's Game of Life, played on the pixels of an image. Each cell is either empty or occupied by one of the species, and every generation applies the same four rules at once:

  • A live cell with fewer than two neighbours dies — solitude.
  • A live cell with more than three neighbours dies — overcrowding.
  • A live cell with two or three neighbours lives on.
  • An empty cell with exactly three neighbours becomes populated.

Neighbours are the eight surrounding cells. Nothing is random and nothing takes turns — the whole board updates from the previous state simultaneously.

How a photo becomes a board

Two separate questions get asked of every pixel. Is it alive? — decided by colourfulness and brightness, so blacks, whites and greys are empty space. Which species? — decided by hue: the strongest of red, green or blue, or the nearest of six hues in 6-species mode.

Colour depth posterises the picture before that judgement — 8, 64, 4096 or 16.7 million colours, always dithered. It does not decide how many species exist; the species count does. With the image palette, the species are the picture's own dominant colours, found by clustering the living pixels.

Cross-species rules

Conway's counts — under two is solitude, over three is crowding, two or three survives, three fills a cell — are the starting point, not a fixed law. What you choose is which neighbours the counts are taken over, and what the counts themselves are:

  • Death — whose presence counts against a living cell: everyone, only kin, friends minus enemies, or a predator species that hunts the rest.
  • Birth — what it takes to fill an empty cell: any three neighbours, three of one species, or three left after enemies cancel friends.
  • Newborn colour — which species appears: the majority parent, a blend of their hues, the rarer or commoner one, a mutation, or nothing at all.
  • Neighbour counts — how many it takes to be born and to survive. B3/S23 is Conway; B36/S23 is HighLife, which has a replicator; B3/S12345 is Maze. Set them per species and one colour can run Maze while another stays Conway, on the same board.

The Preset menu fills the first three in at once for the named combinations, and flips to Custom the moment you change one. Counts are separate — they apply on top of any preset.

For motion work

Turn on motion trails, pick the Keystone predator preset with conversion on, and let it run at high speed — then record. Zoom in first: the recorder captures the board view, so a tight crop at 8× gives a very different film from a whole-image wide shot.
